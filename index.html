Incident Commander Role by Phase

Your responsibilities move through three distinct phases. Preparation builds the foundation, execution tests your command decisions, and evaluation turns the exercise into real improvement.

01 · Before

Preparation

  • Review the AEP — confirm command structure, authorities, and agency roles.
  • Understand scenario & objectives — know the emergency type and what is being tested.
  • Confirm your role — ensure official IC designation and ICP-to-EOC reporting lines.
  • Join planning meetings — help design the exercise around the systems you rely on.
02 · During

Execution

  • Establish command & the ICP — set up a clearly identifiable command location.
  • Conduct size-up — gather information fast, prioritize, task resources.
  • Build & communicate the IAP — state objectives, strategy, critical functions.
  • Assign command staff — Safety Officer, PIO, Liaison Officer.
  • Coordinate multi-agency response — fire, police, EMS, ops, tenants.
03 · After

Evaluation

  • Hot Wash — immediate feedback on what worked and what did not.
  • After Action Report — document strengths, gaps, corrective actions, timelines.
  • Update the AEP — fold lessons learned back into procedures.
💡
Command principle: the IC directs, but does not personally fight the fire. Establish the ICP early, delegate the Safety Officer, PIO and Liaison Officer, and keep your own span of control manageable.

Tabletop Exercise Team Labels

Two distinct sets of labels matter: the staff who run the drill, and the simulated response teams that play in it.

Exercise Staff — Who Runs the Drill

Players / Participants

Actively role-play their real-world positions — including you as IC — and respond to the scenario.

Facilitators

Present scenario updates (injects) and guide discussion. They do not participate in response decisions.

Evaluators / Observers

Subject matter experts who watch and document performance against objectives without intervening.

Note-takers / Scribes

Record the timeline, key decisions, and identified gaps for the After Action Report.

Controllers

Ensure safety and scenario progression as scripted. More common in full-scale exercises.

Simulated Response Teams — Functional Groups

🎯Incident / Unified Command

Core leadership: IC, Safety Officer, Liaison Officer, PIO.

🚒Fire / Rescue & ARFF

Aircraft Rescue and Firefighting plus structural fire response.

🚑EMS / Medical / Triage

Emergency medical services, casualty collection, hospital coordination.

🚔Law Enforcement / Security

Airport police, perimeter control, scene security.

🛫Airport Operations / Airside

Runway closures, NOTAMs, airfield management.

👨‍👩‍👧Passenger / Family Assistance

Uninjured passenger gathering, Friends & Relatives Center, reunification.

📢Public Information / Media

Press briefings and public messaging.

📦Logistics / Finance

Resource ordering, tracking, cost documentation.

🏢Airlines / Tenant Reps

Airline operations and ground handler coordination.

💡
How to choose labels: match them to your exercise objectives. Use ICS sections (Operations, Planning, Logistics, Finance/Admin) to test internal command. Use department names for smaller single-organization drills. Always mirror the terminology in your AEP.

Key Locations — Airport Reference Layout

Generic airport layout for exercise planning: Runway oriented north–south, parallel taxiway and apron on the west side, passenger terminal beyond the apron, perimeter road along the east boundary, and the airport support zone to the south. Hover any numbered marker to highlight it.

Rename or add locations, adjust zone rings — saved in this browser.
💡
Placement logic: the runway runs north–south with the apron and terminal on the west side. ARFF sits southwest of the apron for rapid runway access. The ICP is positioned upwind (west of the crash site) and outside the warm zone. Triage and CCP are further west, using the apron and ARFF zone. The FAC is in the terminal complex, off the operational area. The morgue uses a remote corner of the airfield, well away from the terminal.

Aircraft Incident Zones

The scene is organized into three concentric safety zones, adapted from hazardous materials response. Aircraft accidents involve high fuel loads and composite materials, so the boundaries matter.

Hot Zone

Red / Exclusion Zone

The immediate hazard area: wreckage, active fire, fuel, structural collapse, and hazardous cargo.

Only fully PPE-equipped rescue personnel (e.g. ARFF) enter — and only for life-saving missions. The IC remains outside this zone.

Warm Zone

Yellow / Transition Zone

A controlled buffer between the hazard and the safe area. Hosts triage, casualty collection, decontamination, and PPE donning/doffing.

Access limited to authorized personnel. The Incident Command Post may sit here or just inside the Cold Zone.

Cold Zone

Green / Support Zone

The safe area outside the hazard, free from contamination. Contains the Command Post, staging areas, media briefing area, and the temporary morgue.

This is where the Incident Commander and support staff typically operate.

Perimeters

Inner Cordon

Controls access to the immediate hazard area — roughly the Hot / Warm boundary.

Outer Cordon

A larger secure perimeter for emergency vehicle movement and crowd control, encompassing the whole operation.

Types of Airport Emergencies

Categorizing potential emergencies helps you test specific response plans and resource coordination. Frameworks from ICAO, EASA and the FAA group them broadly like this.

✈️

Aircraft-Related

  • Accidents on or off the airport
  • In-flight emergencies — Full Emergency and Local Standby
  • Gear-up landing, disabled aircraft on runway
  • Ground collisions and taxiway incidents
🏢

Non-Aircraft

  • Structural fires — terminal, hangar, cargo
  • Fuel farm / storage fires
  • HazMat / Dangerous Goods spills
  • Radiological incidents
  • Utility failures (e.g. movement area lighting)
  • Water rescue situations (airport perimeter)
🔒

Security & Unlawful Interference

  • Sabotage and bomb threats
  • Unlawfully seized aircraft (hijack)
  • Armed attacks and hostage situations
  • Acts of sabotage at airport installations
🩺

Public Health & Environmental

  • Communicable disease spread via air travel
  • Severe outbreak affecting airport staff
  • Natural disasters — earthquake, flood, tornado
  • Volcanic ash events
⚡

Compound Emergencies

  • Two or more emergencies simultaneously or in quick succession
  • Example: aircraft accident + fuel fire + HazMat spill
  • Excellent stress test for your Incident Command System and resource management

Fuel & Airport Considerations

⛽
Check your airport's fuel storage and refuelling capability. Some smaller airports have no aviation fuel storage or refuelling facility on-site. Aircraft arriving must carry sufficient fuel for the return leg, or fuel must be pre-positioned by the operator.

Consider how your national supply chain works: where is jet fuel stored, how is it distributed to your airport, and what happens if that supply chain is disrupted during an emergency?
💡
Exercise injects this enables: "Aircraft declares minimum fuel and requests priority landing — no fuel available for turn-around." Or "Operator requests emergency fuel bowser delivery by road/sea." Both test your logistics and liaison functions hard.

❔ User Guide — How to Use This App

A browser-based tool for running airport emergency tabletop exercises (TTX). Everything runs locally — no account, no server — and your data stays on this device.

Quick start Screen layout The 7 sections Scenarios & timeline Header tools Interactive map Checklist Shortcuts Saving & backup Mobile & touch Offline install Print & exports

🚀 Quick start

  1. Pick a scenario — choose one in the Scenario dropdown in the header. The scenario panel (key facts & injects) and the Exercise Timeline appear at the top of the page.
  2. Review the reference tabs — use the 7 tabs below the header to walk through your IC role, team labels, the airport map, incident zones, emergency types and aircraft specs.
  3. Run the exercise — log injects and decisions in the Timeline, check off the IC Checklist, and keep Resources and Casualties up to date as the play unfolds.
  4. Wrap up — export the 📋 After Action Report (.md), review History, back up with Data, then Print or export the PDF package.

🧭 Screen layout

  • Header (top) — app title plus the tool buttons. It scrolls away as you read, giving the content full space.
  • Scenario row — the dropdown that selects the active exercise scenario.
  • Tab bar — the 7 sections. It stays pinned to the top of the screen while you scroll. On phones it scrolls sideways and keeps the active tab in view.
  • Main content — everything belonging to the selected section.
  • Bottom dock — app info, live scenario count, shortcut hints, and the Guide · GitHub · Tests links.

📝 Scenarios & the Exercise Timeline

  • 13 built-in scenarios cover a range of emergency types — just pick one from the dropdown.
  • 📝 Scenarios (Ctrl+E) opens the scenario editor: create your own with name, aircraft, souls on board, fuel load, fire involvement, category, estimated casualties (red / yellow / green / deceased), required resources (ARFF, ambulances, fire trucks, buses) and exercise injects. Custom scenarios are saved on your device and appear in the dropdown.
  • Exercise Timeline (appears once a scenario is selected):
    • Add events: time, description and a category — Info, Inject, Decision, Milestone or Casualty (colour-coded).
    • Delete a single event with its × button.
    • ↩ Undo / ↪ Redo (Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y).
    • Clear All Events, or Export for AAR to download the log as a timestamped .txt file.
  • ⏱ Exercise clock — Start / Pause / Reset a live T+ clock for the exercise. It survives page reloads, shows each inject's release moment as a T+ badge, and can release injects automatically on schedule (or one at a time with ▶). Released injects are logged to the timeline with a sound cue.
  • 📋 After Action Report (button under the timeline) — a live report combining checklist progress, the full timeline table, resource status, casualty triage totals and a blank improvement plan. Type facilitator notes (saved as you type), then Download .md, Copy Markdown or Print.

🧰 Header tools

  • ❔ Guide — this help page, always available here and from the dock.
  • 📝 Scenarios — scenario editor (Ctrl+E): build and manage custom scenarios.
  • 📋 ICS Forms — seven styled forms: ICS 201 Briefing, 202 Objectives, 203 Organization, 204 Assignment List, 205 Communications List, 206 Medical Plan and 209 Incident Summary — auto-filled from the scenario, timeline and trackers. Print them, download as .txt, or copy to clipboard.
  • 🚒 Resources — track the 10 default units (ARFF, EMS, fire, transport, security, medical) with status, location and notes. Export a status report or reset everything.
  • 🚑 Casualties — log tagged casualties with triage category, location, notes and transported flag; export a casualty report or clear all.
  • 🌤️ Weather — current weather for any place worldwide (Open-Meteo; needs internet): temperature, feels-like, wind, humidity and pressure.
  • 📄 PDF — builds a complete exercise package (scenario, ICS forms, resources, casualties, checklist) and opens the print dialog — choose "Save as PDF".
  • 💾 Data — export everything (or scenarios only) as JSON, and import a file to restore or move data to another device.
  • 📜 History — a timestamped audit trail of the last 50 changes per scenario.
  • 🖨️ Print — prints the whole plan (Ctrl+P); header, tabs and dock are left out of printouts.
  • ☀️ / 🌙 — light/dark theme toggle (Ctrl+T); your choice is remembered.

🗺️ Interactive map (Key Locations tab)

  • Drag the numbered pins — and the TWR and ARFF station markers — with a mouse or a finger to match your airport's layout.
  • Drag the crash site and zone rings together as one group.
  • The legend explains each pin category and includes reset buttons to restore the defaults.
  • ✏️ Customize Map — rename, recategorise, delete and add locations, and tune the zone ring radii for both the map and the zone diagram.
  • Export / Import JSON — move a layout to another device or share it with a co-facilitator; Reset restores the factory defaults (with confirmation).
  • Positions, layout and zone sizes are saved automatically on this device.

✅ Exercise flow & IC checklist (last tab)

  • The Exercise Flow shows the 10-step sequence: Alert → Size-Up → ICP → IAP → Resources → Zones → Casualties → Family Assistance → Media → Recovery & AAR.
  • Check off each of the 11 IC checklist items as it is completed; the progress bar and counter track completion.
  • Reset starts over — and your ticks are saved in the browser between sessions.

💾 Saving, privacy & backup

  • No account, no server — everything is stored in this browser on this device.
  • Same device and browser = your scenarios, checklist, timeline, map layout & zone sizes, trackers, clock and facilitator notes persist after you close the tab.
  • Clearing site data — or switching browser/device — erases it, so export regularly.
  • 💾 Data → Export All creates a JSON backup; Import restores it anywhere.
  • 📜 History keeps an audit trail of scenario changes.

📱 Mobile & touch

  • The tool buttons become one swipeable row under the title; the tab bar scrolls sideways and auto-scrolls so the active tab is always visible.
  • Buttons, tabs and checklist items use larger tap targets.
  • Drag the map pins with your finger — dragging never scrolls the page.
  • Form fields use larger text so iOS Safari doesn't zoom when you tap them.

📲 Offline & installing

  • Works offline — after the first visit the dashboard loads with no internet connection (airport ops networks are unreliable). Weather is the only feature that needs a connection.
  • Install as an app — use your browser's Install app / Add to Home Screen for a home-screen icon and a full-screen window.
  • Updates — when a new version is published a small reload toast appears; one tap installs it.

🖨️ Printing & exports

  • 🖨️ Print — the full plan in a clean paper layout (app chrome hidden).
  • 📄 PDF — a monochrome "complete exercise package" for the record.
  • Print (ICS / AAR) — the ICS forms and the After Action Report print on their own, clean and monochrome, without the app around them.
  • .txt / .md exports — timeline, resource status and casualty report as .txt; ICS forms as .txt; the After Action Report as Markdown (.md).
  • JSON exports — full data or scenarios only, for backup and transfer (💾 Data).
